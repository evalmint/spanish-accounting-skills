## 1. Sources and scope

| Source | What it says | Validity |

| PGC 2007 NRV 14ª (RD 1514/2007, rewritten by RD 1/2021) | Control-based model, five steps, over time vs point in time, costs as inventory until transfer | Years beginning on or after 1 January 2021 |

The old PGC 2007 wording ("porcentaje de realización" for services, risks-and-rewards for goods) no longer applies to years from 2021, though the term *porcentaje de realización* survives in practice.

## 2. The five steps

NRV 14.1; RICAC arts. 3-20.

1. **Identify the contract** (art. 3): commitment, identifiable rights and payment terms, commercial substance, probable collection. Combine contracts negotiated together, priced interdependently or forming a single obligation (art. 4). A portfolio approach is allowed when the result does not differ materially (art. 1.4).
2. **Identify the performance obligations** (arts. 6-7): a good or service is distinct if the customer can benefit from it alone or with readily available resources **and** it is separately identifiable in the contract (no significant integration service, no significant modification of other items, not highly interdependent).
3. **Determine the transaction price** (arts. 12-15): fixed plus variable consideration, adjusted for a significant financing component.
4. **Allocate the price** to the obligations by stand-alone selling prices (arts. 16-19).
5. **Recognise revenue** when or as each obligation is satisfied (arts. 8-11).

A project contract often contains several obligations: design, build, licence, support, warranty beyond assurance. Each gets its own timing.

## 3. Over time: the three criteria

NRV 14.2.1; RICAC art. 9. Any one is enough:

- **a)** The customer simultaneously receives and consumes the benefits as the company performs (another provider would not need to redo the work done).
- **b)** The work creates or improves an asset the customer controls as it is created (construction on the customer's land).
- **c)** The asset has **no alternative use** to the company (contractual or practical restriction, art. 9.2) **and** the company has an **enforceable right to payment for performance completed to date**.

Criterion c) is where most software and engineering contracts land. The right to payment must cover costs plus a reasonable margin for the work done if the customer terminates for reasons other than the company's breach; read the termination clause, not the invoicing schedule.

## 4. Point in time: the indicators

NRV 14.2.2; RICAC art. 10: the customer has the significant risks and rewards; physical possession (beware bill-and-hold and consignment); acceptance (a formality only if compliance can be determined objectively; otherwise wait for acceptance); a present right to payment; legal title. Until then, the costs of producing the good or service are **inventory** (NRV 14.2), typically **330** *Productos en curso* or **350** *Productos terminados*, with the change booked through **710**/**712** at closing.

## 5. Measuring progress (grado de avance)

RICAC art. 11.

- **One method per obligation**, and the same method for similar obligations (art. 11.2).
- **Output methods** (art. 11.4): direct measures of value transferred: surveys of work performed (*certificaciones periciales*), milestones, units delivered, time elapsed. Not suitable if they miss work in progress the customer already controls. Practical expedient: if the company is entitled to invoice an amount that matches the value of work done (for example a fixed rate per hour), it may recognise revenue for that amount.
- **Input methods** (art. 11.5): costs incurred, labour hours, machine hours, time elapsed, relative to the total expected. Exclude inputs that do not depict transfer: costs that are not *coste de producción* under the ICAC 2015 resolution, and wasted or abnormal costs. For uninstalled materials bought from a third party, not distinct, controlled by the customer well before the related service and significant in cost, recognise revenue equal to their cost (zero margin) and keep them out of the progress measure (art. 11.5.b).
- **Update** at least at each year-end; changes are changes in estimates, booked prospectively (art. 11.6).

| Method | Formula | Good for | Watch out |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cost-to-cost | Costs incurred / total estimated costs | Construction, fixed-price builds | Front-loaded purchases inflate progress |
| Hours | Hours to date / total estimated hours | Consulting, software builds | Estimate to complete must be refreshed |
| Milestones | Value of milestones reached / total | Clear deliverables | Long gaps between milestones misstate interim revenue |
| Surveys / certificaciones | Value certified / contract value | Construction | A customer certificate is evidence, not automatically the measure |
| Straight-line time | Days elapsed / contract days | Even-effort services | Only if effort really is even |

## 6. When progress cannot be measured

Early in a contract, if progress cannot be measured reasonably but the company expects to recover its costs, recognise revenue **equal to costs incurred** (zero margin) until it can (NRV 14.2). If recovery is not expected, recognise the costs as expenses and consider the onerous contract provision.

## 7. Contract modifications and claims

RICAC art. 5. A change in scope or price agreed by the parties, even if the price is still in dispute (estimate it as variable consideration).

| Modification | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Adds distinct goods/services at their stand-alone price | Separate contract (art. 5.2) |
| Remaining goods/services distinct from those already transferred | Terminate and create a new contract prospectively (art. 5.3.a) |
| Remaining work is part of a single partially satisfied obligation | Cumulative catch-up adjustment to revenue (art. 5.3.b) |

Claims and change orders (*reclamaciones*, *modificados*) enter the price only when enforceable and within the variable consideration constraint.

## 8. Variable consideration, financing, principal vs agent

- **Variable consideration** (art. 13): bonuses, penalties, incentives, price revisions. Include the best estimate only to the extent it is **highly probable** that no significant reversal will occur. Penalties for delay usually reduce the price.
- **Significant financing component** (art. 14): adjust when the payment timing gives a significant financing benefit; not when the customer prepaid and timing is at its discretion, or for other listed exceptions.
- **Principal or agent** (art. 26): gross revenue only if the company controls the good or service before transfer; subcontracted work the company integrates is usually principal.

## 9. Expected losses

When the unavoidable costs of a contract exceed the economic benefits expected, recognise a provision for the onerous contract under NRV 15ª (PGC account **4994** *Provisión por contratos onerosos*, expense **6954**, release **79544**). Before provisioning, write down any contract cost assets and inventory of that contract (RICAC art. 23.3). Book it in the period the loss becomes probable, regardless of progress. Not deductible for corporate tax until applied (LIS art. 14.3.b; see `tax-and-grants.md`).

## 10. Contract costs

- **Incremental costs of obtaining a contract** (sales commissions payable only if the contract is won): recognised in profit and loss systematically with the transfer of the related goods/services, so deferred if recoverable (RICAC art. 21). Costs incurred regardless of winning are expenses.
- **Costs to fulfil** a contract that create or enhance a resource used to satisfy it (set-up, mobilisation): inventory if part of the operating cycle, otherwise intangible (art. 22); expensed systematically (art. 22.3); impairment test against remaining consideration less costs (art. 23).
- Bidding costs (*costes de licitación*) before the contract is probable: expense.

## 11. Presentation and notes

RICAC art. 33:
- An **unconditional right** to consideration is a receivable in B.III *Deudores comerciales*.
- Revenue recognised before an unconditional right (work done, not yet billable) is still presented in B.III, with separate disclosure in the notes (art. 33.2). In the books: **4309** *Clientes, facturas pendientes de formalizar*.
- Payments received or due before transfer are a **contract liability** in C.V.7 *Anticipos de clientes* (**438**) if short term, or B.V *Periodificaciones a largo plazo* if long term (**181** *Anticipos recibidos por ventas o prestaciones de servicios a largo plazo*) (art. 33.3).
- Deferred costs of obtaining contracts: B.VI *Periodificaciones a corto plazo* (art. 33.5).
- Profit and loss presentation of revenue and of the financing component: RICAC art. 34.
- Notes: revenue disaggregation, contract balances, remaining obligations and judgements, per the *memoria* model of the PGC as amended by RD 1/2021; abbreviated and PYMES models ask for less.

## 12. Worked example

Fixed-price software build, 200,000 euros, over time (criterion c: bespoke, termination clause pays work done plus margin). Budget 150,000 euros of cost. Billing: 30 % on signing, 40 % at beta, 30 % on acceptance.

| Year-end | Costs to date | Estimate to complete | Progress (cost-to-cost) | Cumulative revenue | Billed to date | Balance |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Year 1 | 60,000 | 90,000 | 40 % | 80,000 | 60,000 (30 %) | Contract asset 20,000 (**4309**) |
| Year 2 (overrun) | 150,000 | 60,000 | 71.4 % | 142,857 | 140,000 (70 %) | Contract asset 2,857; total cost 210,000 > 200,000: loss 10,000 overall. Cumulative result so far is 142,857 − 150,000 = −7,143; provide the remaining −2,857 now (**6954**/**4994**) |

Entries are in `accounts-and-entries.md`.
