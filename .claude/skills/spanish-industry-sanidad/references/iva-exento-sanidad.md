# Healthcare: the IVA exemption and its limits

Which healthcare supplies are exempt from IVA in Spain, which are not, and how to invoice them. Statutory text checked against the consolidated LIVA (Ley 37/1992), RIVA (RD 1624/1992) and RD 1619/2012 on boe.es on 24 November 2026. DGT doctrine is cited by principle; where a specific consulta was not checked, it says so.

## 1. The three exemptions

| Exemption | Scope | Limits written into the law |
|---|---|---|
| **Art. 20.Uno.2.º** Hospitalisation and healthcare by public-law bodies, or by private entities or establishments *en régimen de precios autorizados o comunicados*, and services directly related (food, accommodation, operating theatre, medicines and medical supplies used in the establishment) | Hospitals, clinics, laboratories, sanatoriums | Not exempt: medicines delivered for consumption outside; food and accommodation for persons other than patients and their companions; veterinary services; lettings by these entities |
| **Art. 20.Uno.3.º** Assistance to individuals by medical or health professionals, *whoever the recipient of the service is* | Medical, surgical and health assistance for diagnosis, prevention and treatment of illness, including clinical analyses and radiology | Professional must be recognised as a health professional by Spanish law, or be a psychologist, speech therapist or optician with an official qualification |
| **Art. 20.Uno.5.º** Services of stomatologists, dentists, dental mechanics and dental technicians within their profession, and the supply, repair and fitting of dental prostheses and orthodontic appliances by them, whoever pays | Dental clinics and dental laboratories | Covers the prostheses themselves when supplied by these professionals; aesthetic dentistry still faces the purpose test (§3) |

*Precios autorizados o comunicados* means prices whose change requires prior authorisation by, or notice to, a public body (RIVA art. 4). A private clinic that does not meet this test can still exempt the services its health professionals provide to patients under art. 20.Uno.3.º, because that exemption depends on the service and the provider's qualification, not on the legal form (CJEU C-141/00 *Kügler*; DGT applies it to companies). Related services such as accommodation fall back to art. 20.Uno.2.º and need the price regime (unverified: confirm with the clinic's adviser how accommodation in a private clinic without notified prices is treated).

Ambulance transport of patients in specially adapted vehicles is exempt under art. 20.Uno.15.º.

## 2. Who is a health professional

Art. 20.Uno.3.º refers to those "considerados como tales en el ordenamiento jurídico", which points to the Ley 44/2003 de ordenación de las profesiones sanitarias (LOPS), plus psychologists, speech therapists and opticians with official qualifications named in the LIVA itself.

| Professional | Exempt healthcare? | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Doctors, dentists, pharmacists | Yes, for healthcare acts | LOPS art. 2 |
| Nurses, physiotherapists, occupational therapists, podiatrists, optometrists, speech therapists, dietitian-nutritionists | Yes, for healthcare acts | LOPS art. 2 |
| Técnicos superiores and técnicos sanitarios (FP), such as dental hygienists or lab technicians | Yes when acting within their competence (unverified for services billed directly to patients without a supervising dentist or doctor) | LOPS art. 3 |
| Psychologists | Yes, for diagnosis and treatment (psychotherapy); not for HR selection, coaching, team training or court expert reports | LIVA art. 20.Uno.3.º |
| Veterinarians | No: veterinary services are excluded | art. 20.Uno.2.º c; farm-animal services 10 % (art. 91.Uno.2.3.º), pets 21 % |
| Osteopaths, naturopaths, acupuncturists, homeopaths without a recognised health qualification | No: 21 % | Not a recognised health profession; if performed by a doctor or physiotherapist with therapeutic purpose, exempt |
| Beauticians, massage therapists without a physiotherapy degree | No: 21 % | — |

Check the professional's college membership (*número de colegiado*) where the profession requires it.

## 3. The purpose test: therapeutic or not

The CJEU reads the exemption by the **main purpose** of the service: to protect, maintain or restore health (C-307/01 *d'Ambrumenil*, C-212/01 *Unterpertinger*). Cosmetic treatments are exempt only if they treat or provide care to persons who need it because of illness, injury or a congenital physical impairment, including psychological conditions, and the subjective view of the patient is not decisive (C-91/12 *PFC Clinic*). The DGT applies this per treatment and requires evidence.

What evidence to keep, per patient:
- the medical indication and diagnosis in the clinical record (historia clínica);
- who assessed it (a doctor), before the treatment;
- for reconstructive work, the link to the illness or injury.

Without it, AEAT treats the treatment as aesthetic and taxable.

## 5. Who invoices whom: doctors, clinics, insurers, staff hire

| Arrangement | IVA | IRPF retention |
|---|---|---|
| Self-employed doctor treats the clinic's patients and invoices the clinic | Exempt: art. 20.Uno.3.º applies whoever the recipient is | Clinic withholds 15 % (7 % in the first three years of professional activity) on the invoice (RIRPF art. 95.1) |
| Doctor's own company (SL) invoices the clinic | Exempt if the service is healthcare performed by health professionals | No retention on invoices from an SL |
| Clinic hires out a nurse or doctor to another clinic (cesión de personal) | 21 %: staff hire is not healthcare (unverified for hire between hospitals under art. 20.Uno.2.º related-services reading) | — |
| Private health insurer pays the clinic or doctor for insured patients | Exempt (the insurer is a recipient; the patient receives the care) | Insurer withholds 15 % on payments to individual professionals |
| Clinic re-invoices consumables to a doctor who uses its premises | Accessory to a letting or a separate supply, 21 % | — |
| Doctor pays the clinic a percentage of fees for using its facilities | Consideration for premises and services, 21 % | — |

Ask for the contract between doctor and clinic. The contract decides who the patient's counterparty is, who bears clinical responsibility, and so which of the rows above applies.

## 6. Goods sold by clinics, pharmacies and opticians

| Product | IVA | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Medicines for human use, galenic forms, formulas | 4 % | art. 91.Dos.1.3.º |
| Prostheses, orthoses and internal implants for persons with disability (33 %+) | 4 % | art. 91.Dos.1.5.º |
| Glasses, frames for graded glasses, graded contact lenses and products for their use and care | 10 % | art. 91.Uno.1.6.º c and annex 8 |
| Devices for diabetes, incontinence, mobility and other items in annex 8, designed for personal use of persons with disabilities | 10 % | art. 91.Uno.1.6.º c and annex 8 |
| Other pharmaceutical products of chapter 30 of the Combined Nomenclature for direct consumer use | 10 % | art. 91.Uno.1.6.º a |
| Cosmetics, supplements, toothpaste, sun cream, non-graded sunglasses | 21 % | art. 90 |
| Medicines and supplies used during exempt treatment in the establishment | Exempt as part of the service | art. 20.Uno.2.º |

An optician's eye test is exempt (optician named in art. 20.Uno.3.º); the glasses sold after it are 10 %. Invoice them as two lines.

Retailers selling to consumers who are individuals may fall under the *recargo de equivalencia* (equivalence surcharge) regime for the product sales; pharmacies do. See `spanish-vat`.

## 7. Invoicing exempt services

- Invoices **are required** for exempt healthcare and dental services: RD 1619/2012 art. 3.1.a lists art. 20.Uno.2.º, 3.º, 4.º and 5.º among the exempt operations that must still be invoiced.
- The invoice must cite the exemption, for example "Operación exenta de IVA, art. 20.Uno.3.º Ley 37/1992" (RD 1619/2012 art. 6.1.j).
- A simplified invoice is possible up to 400 € (art. 4.1.a). Health services are not in the 3,000 € list (beauty institutes are, art. 4.2.h). Patients claiming reimbursement from insurers usually need a full invoice with their name and NIF.
- Description: use a generic concept ("consulta", "tratamiento de fisioterapia", "sesión de psicoterapia"); avoid diagnoses on invoices, which are processed by accounting staff and software providers (see `actividad-mixta-y-prorrata.md` §8).
- Professionals with IRPF retention show the 15 % or 7 % retention on invoices to businesses (clinics, insurers); not on invoices to patients.
- Exempt invoices issued with software fall under Verifactu (RD 1007/2023 applies to the invoicing system, not to the IVA status).

## 8. Decision checklist

1. Is the provider a recognised health professional, psychologist, speech therapist, optician, dentist or dental technician, or a hospital or clinic with the price regime?
2. Is the main purpose of the service the patient's health, and is it documented per patient?
3. Is it really healthcare to the patient, or staff hire, room letting, a report for a third party, teaching or a product?
4. If a product is sold, which rate: 4 %, 10 % or 21 %?
5. Issue an invoice in every case, citing the exemption where applicable.
6. If anything in the clinic is taxable, go to `actividad-mixta-y-prorrata.md`.
