## 1. Capitalising research, development and software

PGC NRV 6ª.a and 6ª.e:

| Item | Rule |
|---|---|
| Research | Expense, but **may** be capitalised from the moment both conditions are met; amortised within **5 years** |
| Development | **Must** be recognised as an asset when both conditions are met; amortised over useful life, presumed not to exceed **5 years** unless proved otherwise |
| Conditions | (1) specifically individualised by project with costs clearly established; (2) well-founded reasons for technical success and economic-commercial profitability |
| Doubts arise later | Amounts in the asset are written off to profit and loss at once |
| Software for own use or sale, bought or developed in-house, including website development | Asset in **206** *Aplicaciones informáticas*, with the same recognition and amortisation criteria as development |
| Maintenance of software | Never capitalised |

Other rules: an intangible whose useful life cannot be estimated reliably is amortised over **10 years** (PGC NRV 5ª, as amended by RD 602/2016); start-up costs of the company or of a new line of business are never intangible assets (Resolución ICAC 28-05-2013). Capitalised development becomes *propiedad industrial* when a patent or similar is obtained (NRV 6ª.b).

For a SaaS company: a new platform or major new module with project records, a technical feasibility point and a business case → capitalise development costs incurred after both conditions are met. Bug fixing, minor updates and running the service → expense. Under the PGC PYMES the same NRV 6 criteria apply.

What goes in the cost: direct personnel (salaries and employer social security of the developers, by timesheet), contractors, and a reasonable share of indirect costs attributable to the project; not general administration, selling or training costs.

## 2. Entries and amortisation

During the year, costs are booked by nature (**640**, **642**, **607**, **623**). At period end, capitalise the qualifying amount through income:

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---|---|
| **201** Desarrollo (or **206** Aplicaciones informáticas) | 180,000 | |
| **730** Trabajos realizados para el inmovilizado intangible | | 180,000 |

Amortisation from when the asset is available for use, straight-line over ≤ 5 years:

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---|---|
| **680** Amortización del inmovilizado intangible | 36,000 | |
| **2801** Amortización acumulada de desarrollo (or **2806**) | | 36,000 |

Impairment tests when indicators exist; write-off immediately if success or profitability becomes doubtful. Notes: amounts capitalised, amortisation, and the reasons for capitalising (Resolución ICAC 2013, notes requirements).

## 3. Tax treatment of capitalised development

- **Free amortisation** for tax of R&D expenses capitalised as intangible assets, excluding amortisation of elements that already enjoy free amortisation (LIS art. 12.3.c). The company may deduct the whole capitalised amount in the year, booking a deferred tax liability (**479**) for the difference.
- Software (**206**) without R&D character: the table coefficient for *sistemas y programas informáticos* is up to **33 %**, maximum 6 years (LIS art. 12.1.a).
- New tangible fixed assets (hardware) of unit value ≤ 300 euros: free amortisation up to 25,000 euros a period (art. 12.3.e). Other accelerated regimes for *empresas de reducida dimensión*: see `spanish-corporate-tax`.
- Development cost capitalised and also used as the base of the art. 35 deduction: the deduction base is the expense of the period, including amortisation of assets used in R&D; avoid counting the same cost twice (once as expense, once as amortisation).

## 4. The dividend restriction

No profit may be distributed unless available reserves are at least equal to the R&D expenses still shown in the balance sheet (LSC art. 273.3). A startup that capitalises heavily and then makes a profit cannot pay dividends until reserves cover the net capitalised amount. *Empresas emergentes* must not distribute dividends at all to keep the status (Ley 28/2022 art. 3.1.c).

## 5. I+D+i deduction (LIS art. 35)

**R&D** (*investigación y desarrollo*, art. 35.1.a) includes the creation, combination and configuration of **advanced software** through new theorems and algorithms, operating systems, languages, interfaces and applications aimed at new or substantially improved products, processes or services. Routine maintenance and minor updates of software are excluded.

**Technological innovation** (*innovación tecnológica*, IT, art. 35.2, rewritten by RDL 40/2026): activity resulting in a technological advance in new or substantially improved products or processes, including technological diagnosis, industrial design and process engineering, acquisition of advanced technology (patents, licences, know-how, designs; base capped at 1 M euros, not from related parties) and ISO-type certification.

Exclusions (art. 35.3): activities without significant scientific or technological novelty, routine adaptations to a client's requirements, production and service provision, and more.

| Deduction | Rate | Source |
|---|---|---|
| R&D expenses | **25 %** up to the average of the two previous years; **42 %** on the excess | art. 35.1.c.1º |
| Qualified researchers exclusively dedicated to R&D | Additional **17 %** of their personnel cost | art. 35.1.c.1º |
| Investments in R&D fixed assets (not buildings or land) | **8 %** | art. 35.1.c.2º |
| Technological innovation | **12 %**; **15 %** in the first two periods with IT expenses, in periods where they grow at least 20 %, or where they are not below the average of the previous two | art. 35.2.c, as amended by RDL 40/2026 |

**What changed on 21 November 2026.** RDL 40/2026, de 20 de noviembre (BOE-A-2026-24437, in force on publication, 21-11-2026) raised the IT rate from 12 % to 15 % in those cases, rewrote the IT concept and base, allows an order to specify IT activities, and changed monetisation (§6). The text states no specific tax-period effect for these LIS changes; which periods they cover is not settled here (unverified). A royal decree-law must be convalidated by Congress within 30 days; check its status before relying on it.

The base is reduced by grants received for the same activities and imputed to income in the period (art. 35.1.b and 35.2.b). Expenses must be for activities carried out in the EU or EEA and individually tracked by project.

## 6. Using the deduction: limits, carry-forward, monetisation

- **Limit:** deductions of Chapter IV together cannot exceed **25 %** of the cuota íntegra (after double-taxation deductions and bonificaciones), raised to **50 %** when R&D and IT deductions for the period's own expenses exceed 10 % of that cuota (art. 39.1).
- **Carry-forward:** **18 years** for art. 35 deductions (15 years for the others); for *entidades de nueva creación* and companies that restore losses with new contributions, the clock can start in the first year with positive results (art. 39.1).
- **Monetisation** (art. 39.2, as amended by RDL 40/2026): companies taxed under art. 29.1 or 29.6 may take R&D and IT deductions outside the limit with a **20 % discount**, and request a cash refund if the cuota is insufficient. Cap: **3 M euros** a year per group for R&D and IT together, plus **2 M euros** extra for R&D when R&D expenses exceed **10 % of turnover**. Conditions: at least one year since the end of the period that generated it; average headcount (total or in R&D/IT) not reduced; at least **80 %** of the amount reinvested in R&D/IT expenses or exclusively dedicated assets within **24 months**; an **informe motivado** or an acuerdo previo de valoración. Breach means regularisation (art. 125.3).

## 7. Informe motivado and acuerdos previos

- *Informe motivado*: a report from the Ministry of Science (through accredited certification of the project, RD 1432/2003) qualifying the activity as R&D or IT and its expenses. It binds the AEAT on the scientific-technological qualification (art. 35.4.a). Required for monetisation; strongly advisable otherwise.
- *Acuerdo previo de valoración* with the AEAT on the expenses of R&D/IT projects (art. 35.4.c).
- Typical sequence: project definition → certification of the project (ex ante or ex post) by an ENAC-accredited body → informe motivado → apply the deduction in modelo 200.

## 8. Social security bonus for researchers

RD 475/2014, as amended by RDL 40/2026 art. 31 (in force 21-11-2026):
- **Indefinite** hiring of staff dedicated **exclusively and full time** to R&D or IT (as defined in LIS art. 35) gives a **40 %** bonus on the employer's contribution for contingencias comunes for up to **5 years**.
- Plus **5 %** for researchers under 30 and **5 %** for women researchers, cumulative.
- Up to **15 %** of time on training, teaching or outreach still counts as exclusive dedication.
- The decree regulates its compatibility with the art. 35 deduction (RD 475/2014 art. 1 as amended); check the current compatibility rule before combining both for the same person (unverified detail).
- Predoctoral contracts: 200 euros a month bonus (RDL 1/2023 art. 27, as amended by RDL 40/2026).

## 9. Patent box for advanced registered software (LIS art. 23)

- Reduction of the tax base for income from licensing (and from sales to unrelated parties) of patents, utility models, protected designs and **advanced registered software derived from R&D** (art. 23.1).
- Reduction = **60 %** × (qualifying own expenses × 1.3, capped at total expenses) / (total expenses including related-party subcontracting and acquisition) (art. 23.1).
- Income = revenue less the related expenses not capitalised, art. 12.2 amounts and other related expenses (art. 23.2).
- Conditions (art. 23.3): the licensee uses it in an economic activity (with an anti-abuse rule for related parties); the licensee is not in a tax haven unless EU with valid reasons; accessory services in the same contract priced separately; records allowing income and expenses per asset.
- SaaS revenue (access to a hosted service) is generally not "licensing a right of use" of the software; patent box fits licensing models. Get advice and consider an acuerdo previo de valoración.

## 10. Empresas emergentes

Certified startups (Ley 28/2022; see `spanish-start-company`, `references/startup-law.md`): **15 %** IS in the first profitable period and the next three (art. 7), deferral without guarantees of the first two years' IS (art. 8.1), no pagos fraccionados (art. 8.2). Compare with the ordinary 2026 rates: 25 % general; 19 % / 21 % for turnover under 1 M euros; 23 % for *reducida dimensión*; 15 % *nueva creación* for two years (LIS art. 29.1, DT 44ª). The R&D deduction applies on top, subject to the limits above.

## 11. Employee equity: stock options and phantom shares

**IRPF for employees** (Ley 28/2022 DF 3ª, from 1 January 2023):
- Shares delivered to employees, including on exercise of options: exempt up to **12,000 euros** a year generally, **50,000 euros** for employees of an *empresa emergente*; startup conditions tested at **grant** of the option; the offer need only fit the company's general pay policy (LIRPF art. 42.3.f). (The exemption is in art. 42.3.f, not art. 7.)
- Taxable excess on emergente shares: imputed on listing, on disposal, or at the latest after **10 years** (LIRPF art. 14.2.m).
- Value: price paid by an independent investor in the last capital increase of the previous year, else market value (LIRPF art. 43.1.1º.g).
- Withholding applies to the taxable payment in kind; see `spanish-payroll`.

**Accounting** (PGC NRV 17ª):
- Equity-settled (shares or options over shares): personnel expense over the vesting period at the grant-date fair value, credited to equity: debit **6450** *Retribuciones al personal liquidados con instrumentos de patrimonio*, credit **1111** *Resto de instrumentos de patrimonio neto*.
- Cash-settled (phantom shares, SARs): expense against a liability re-measured at each closing, **147** *Provisión por transacciones con pagos basados en instrumentos de patrimonio* (expense in **645** sub-accounts).

**Corporate tax:** equity-settled plans are deductible when the shares are delivered (LIS art. 14.6); cash-settled plans are not deductible as a provision and become deductible when paid (art. 14.3.e, 14.5). Both create temporary differences.

## 12. Digital nomads and hiring from abroad

- The international remote work visa (Ley 14/2013 arts. 74 bis-74 quinquies) is for non-EU nationals working remotely for companies outside Spain; a Spanish SaaS company cannot hire them as employees under it, though self-employed holders may bill Spanish clients up to 20 % of their activity. See `spanish-start-company` (`references/startup-law.md`).
- Staff moving to Spain may opt for the impatriados regime (LIRPF art. 93): 24 % flat on employment income up to 600,000 euros for the year of arrival and five more, if not resident in the previous five years. Payroll impact in `spanish-payroll`.
- Hiring a developer abroad without a local entity: permanent establishment and local payroll questions go to an adviser.

## 13. Ask the user

- Does the company want to capitalise development, and does it have project records, timesheets and a business case per project?
- PGC or PGC PYMES, and audited?
- Which projects might qualify as R&D or IT, and are they certified or planned for an informe motivado?
- Any grants for the same projects?
- Researchers on indefinite, exclusive contracts?
- Any licensing of registered software to third parties?
- Certified as an empresa emergente? Since when?
- Option or phantom plans: grant dates, vesting, settlement, valuation?
